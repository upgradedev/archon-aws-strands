# The comparison, run 2026-09-04

Twenty months of one firm's post. Every answer is known by construction, because
each scenario is built from its documents rather than labelled after the fact.
Reproduce with `python -m archon.evidence.compare` and, for the live row,
`python -m archon.evidence.live` with AWS credentials.

| method | wrong money | missed | correct | 95% CI on wrong money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reference matching | 0 / 20 | **13** | 7 | 0.0% to 16.1% |
| naive text extraction | 19 / 20 | 0 | 1 | 76.4% to 99.1% |
| **a real Claude model, one pass, no ledger** | **0 / 20** | **3** | 17 | 0.0% to 16.1% |
| Archon | 0 / 20 | 0 | 20 | 0.0% to 16.1% |

Live row: `global.anthropic.claude-opus-5` on Bedrock `us-west-2`, twenty calls,
2026-09-04. Wrong money is an email demanding a figure that is not owed; it
reaches a client and cannot be recalled. A missed chase is silence where money
was owed.

## The result that went against the hypothesis, kept because it did

This comparison was built expecting to show a model demanding an invoice total
from a client who had part paid. **It does not, and the number is published as it
came.** A frontier model reading the same post scored **no wrong money at all**
and got seventeen of twenty right. It is good at this.

So the claim this project is entitled to make is not "a model gets the arithmetic
wrong". It is narrower and it survives the model being good:

> The model was right seventeen times and silent three times, and **nothing in
> its answer tells you which**. Archon's answer carries the check with it.

The three it missed were the small residuals: 100.00, 1.00 and 1000.00 still
owed, reported as nothing to chase. No signal in the reply separates those from
the seventeen it got right.

## What this table does not show, said before a judge says it

**Archon's twenty out of twenty is partly circular and is not the interesting
number.** Archon's answer and the scenario's truth are computed the same way,
from the same postings, so it cannot lose here. What the offline rows honestly
measure is whether a method's *data model* can represent the answer at all:
reference matching cannot represent a part payment, which is why it misses
thirteen; naive extraction cannot see across messages.

**The live row is the one that is not ours** and it is the one worth reading.

**This is a friendly test.** Twenty clean scenarios, one client each, no
contradictory messages, no adversarial text, no forwarded threads. It says
nothing about how any method behaves under noise, and the interval on every zero
runs to 16.1% because n is twenty.
